What this site is

A demonstration, not an account

Every equity figure, position, trade, rationale and chart on the other pages here is invented. They come from a fixture committed to the repository and generated by a script, and the site makes no network request of any kind. There is no API behind it, because there is nothing for an API to reach.

That is a deliberate line rather than an unfinished feature. The real dashboard renders live brokerage state, and it has no login precisely because it binds to the loopback address and is reachable from nowhere else. Publishing that would put a live view of a brokerage account on the open internet, so it stays where it is. Real authentication would be the prerequisite for changing that, not a follow-up to it.

The limits on the Rules page are the exception: those are copied verbatim from the repository, because they are limits rather than secrets and they are already public.

Story

Written straight

Mudhorn Capital is a private vehicle. It manages one account, for one person, and takes no money from anyone else.

It exists because inherited capital is easy to hold and hard to deserve. The name comes from the practice of granting a signet for a deed: a mark is not chosen, and it is not passed down. It is awarded once something has actually been done. That felt like the right standard to hold a portfolio to.

The method is unglamorous and deliberately so. Position size is decided before conviction. Every trade carries a stop written down in advance. No more than one per cent of the account is at risk on any single position, and no more than two per cent across all of them at once. A run of losses suspends real money automatically, because the worst decisions arrive after the third one.

Most days the correct action is none. The discipline is the product.

Tone of voice

Plain, exact, unhurried

Say the number. Never claim an outcome that has not happened. Nothing that implies managing anyone else's money, because it does not and that phrasing carries a regulatory meaning. Confidence comes from precision, not from adjectives.

  • Risking 0.4% on this one. Invalidated below 575. States the size and the exit before the idea.
  • Three losses in a row, so live trading is off for the rest of the week. Paper carries on. Reports a rule firing without defending or apologising for it.
  • Nothing worth taking today. Doing nothing is a result, and it is reported like one.

Mark directions

Three approaches, not three variations

Each solves the brief differently. The one constant is that none of them draws a creature: a literal beast reads as a mascot, and it disappears entirely at 16 pixels.

Recommended

A. The signet

A ring enclosing a struck peak. Reads as heraldry or a seal to anyone who does not know, which is exactly the register a boutique firm wants. Symmetrical, solid, and the enclosing ring keeps it legible when it shrinks.

B. The brand

A stamped block with the horn cut out as negative space, like a maker's mark burned into a crate. The most industrial of the three and the strongest at very small sizes, because it is almost entirely solid.

C. The monogram

Purely typographic. An M whose central vertex is driven into a point rather than dipping, so the horn is in the letterform itself. No pictorial element at all, which makes it the safest and the least distinctive.

The mark at size

It has to survive a favicon

Everything here is a solid silhouette with no stroke thinner than two pixels at the smallest size. That constraint is why the horn is a struck wedge rather than a curve.

Palette

Cold and mineral

The name begins with "mud", so every warm tone was ruled out early. Brown or tan makes the whole thing read agricultural. This palette is blue-shifted throughout, and the single accent is oxidised copper rather than gold.

The only accent. Focus rings, one hover state, the ring on the mark. Used sparingly enough to still mean something.

Two additions for the interface

A brand page needs one accent. A book of forty trades needs to be scannable for wins against losses without reading every figure, so exactly two colours are added, and they are used only on numbers and on the small state chips beside them. Chrome, links and focus rings still belong to patina, so the accent keeps meaning something.

Figures and data

Every number in a trading interface should be tabular, so columns align and a changing digit does not shift the row. IBM Plex Mono (free, SIL OFL) pairs with the body face by design. Every figure on this site is set with font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums for the same reason.

--mono: ui-monospace, SFMono-Regular, "SF Mono", Menlo, Consolas, "Liberation Mono", monospace;

Tagline
